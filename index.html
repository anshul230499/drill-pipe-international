1

Piercing

A hot billet is pierced and elongated into a seamless hollow tube—the starting point for drill pipe bodies.

The heated billet passes through a piercing mill and rolling operations to establish the required hollow section and dimensions.
2

Upsetting

Induction heating prepares the pipe ends for controlled upsetting and thicker connection zones.

The ends are locally heated and then formed. The photo shows the heating stage, rather than the upsetting press itself.
3

Heat Treatment

Controlled heating, quenching and tempering establish the required mechanical properties.

Quench-and-temper processing is controlled against the specified grade and mechanical performance requirements.
4

Tool-Joint Forging

Forged steel blanks become the foundation of precision-machined pin and box tool joints.

This is a representative steel forging press, not a verified photograph of Jindal tool-joint equipment.
5

Assembly & Inspection

Tool joints are friction-welded, finished and inspected before release.

Friction welding is followed by weld-zone heat treatment, machining, nondestructive inspection and final dimensional checks.
6

Ready for the Rig

Finished drill pipe is prepared for land-based operations, including Permian Basin drilling programs.

The photo shows a modern onshore rig in West Texas. It illustrates the drilling environment without claiming that this is a DPI-supplied project.
